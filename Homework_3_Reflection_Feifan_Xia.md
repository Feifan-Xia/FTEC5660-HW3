## From chatbots to physical systems

The AI news that changed my view in November 2026 came from the physical
layer: NVIDIA's governance crisis, tighter controls on China's access to AI
chips, Huawei's million-chip cluster architecture, Alibaba's T-Head listing,
TSMC's extraordinary demand and Samsung and SK hynix's memory and packaging
investments. These stories made AI feel less like a software category and more
like an industrial system built from fabs, high-bandwidth memory, advanced
packaging, power plants and export licences.

This shift changed how I understand AI sycophancy and AI psychosis. I still see
them in individual chatbot conversations, yet the larger risk appears when
organisations and markets repeat a convenient story until it shapes real
investment. A confident model can reinforce one user's belief. A confident AI
narrative can also reinforce a board, a supply chain and an investment cycle.

## Sycophancy becomes expensive at the hardware layer

AI sycophancy begins with a system optimising for the answer its user wants.
In an organisation, the same behaviour can appear as deference to a dominant
founder, selective presentation of evidence or a board that delays difficult
questions. The NVIDIA crisis made that organisational version concrete.
Huang resigned after an investigation connected his private
relationships to national-security concerns. Investors, technology leaders
and employees then campaigned for his return, while the board rejected the
comeback and created a permanent national-security and export-control
committee. Personal loyalty and strategic dependence had become difficult to
separate.

Hardware turns this social bias into capital expenditure. Optimistic forecasts
become wafer reservations, HBM orders, factory construction, power contracts
and debt. TSMC's October revenue rose about 60 percent on AI demand, which
shows that the demand is real. The same reporting also says TSMC began asking
AI customers for cash upfront or credit limits. That is productive friction in
an industrial form: a supplier can challenge the customer's confidence before
the order becomes a stranded asset.

Huawei and Alibaba illustrate the alternative response. Huawei introduced an
architecture intended to connect up to one million AI chips as one computer
and opened an Ascend 950DT cluster designed to export AI tokens. T-Head filed
for a Hong Kong IPO while Alibaba began training a next-generation model on its
own chips. These developments extend sycophancy beyond the model interface.
An ecosystem can become comfortable with its own language, benchmarks and
success claims unless it continuously tests them against yields, utilisation,
customer demand and costs.

## AI psychosis as a feedback loop

I use AI psychosis to describe a system-level loop in which confident claims
about the future produce actions that appear to confirm those claims. Investors
fund compute, cloud providers buy accelerators, chip suppliers expand capacity,
and the resulting spending is read as evidence that the original forecast was
correct. Clinical questions belong to clinicians. For technology and finance,
the practical danger is a closed loop that loses contact with external limits.

NVIDIA is the clearest example. Its first earnings report after Huang left
showed revenue growth of 106 percent, so the demand story retained strong
evidence. At the same time, the company stopped shipping US-approved AI chips
to China, California opened an investigation into China-related chip flows,
and the stock still faced a severe re-rating. Operational strength and market
belief moved on different clocks. That gap is where collective psychosis
becomes analytically useful: the market can hold two incompatible stories,
switching between invincible demand and systemic fragility.

The supply chain is creating several partly independent realities. NVIDIA,
AMD and Google design different accelerator families. TSMC and Samsung act as
the manufacturing gatekeepers. Samsung and SK hynix control scarce memory and
packaging capacity. Huawei and T-Head are building a China-centred stack while
US rules restrict remote access to American compute. These are not separate
from the global AI trade; they are competing routes through the same physical
bottlenecks.

## What I now consider real evidence

Before this period, I would evaluate an AI company primarily through model
quality, benchmark scores and software adoption. Hardware adds a stronger set
of observable constraints: wafer and packaging capacity, HBM supply, power
contracts, cooling capacity, data-centre utilisation, customer prepayments,
inventory, export licences and concentration among suppliers. These indicators
are slower and less exciting than a product launch, which makes them valuable
as a counterweight to the narrative.

This also changes how I judge an AI agent. A useful infrastructure analyst
should connect claims to physical evidence and show where the evidence stops.
It should separate announced capex from spending, order reservations from
revenue, model capability from utilisable compute and customer intention from a
financed contract. The goal is a decision system that exposes bottlenecks and
contrary signals while preserving sources for review.

## How this changes my career plan

I still want to work in large-model and agent engineering, with a stronger
focus on AI infrastructure and hardware-software co-design. The roles that
interest me combine model systems, evaluation and operational data. I want to
build agents that monitor supply chains, compute capacity, power availability
and export-control changes, then connect those signals to financial and
operational decisions.

In fintech, this points toward AI infrastructure intelligence: due diligence on
data-centre projects, supplier-concentration analysis, capex monitoring and
scenario testing around export controls or memory shortages. A future product
could combine agentic research with live utilisation, delivery and financing
data, producing an auditable view of where an AI investment thesis depends on
physical assumptions.

My personal working method follows the same principle. I will ask for the
strongest counterargument, look for an external constraint and keep a short
record of facts, assumptions and decisions. Hardware has taught me that
reality eventually appears as a delivery date, a power limit, a yield curve or
a payment obligation. I want to build AI systems that bring those signals into
the conversation early.
